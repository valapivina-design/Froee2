Crypto.com Refund: Scam, or a Withdrawal That Never Landed

People type three searches for the same afternoon: crypto.com refund, crypto.com scam, crypto.com withdrawal. Crypto.com is a real app and exchange. Their help page on scams says that a cryptocurrency transfer to a third party cannot be halted, and that there is no way to cancel, reverse, or refund it. Their Send article says that once a transfer is complete they cannot cancel or reverse it, and they cannot undo it or recover the cash or the crypto. The file that can still be reviewed is the one where the coins left for someone else.

Which search is yours

Crypto.com refund, Crypto.com scam, Crypto.com withdrawal

  • Crypto.com refund. Two pages, both theirs. The scam guide: no cancel, no reverse, no refund of a cryptocurrency transfer to a third party. The Send article: a completed transfer is final, and Crypto.com is unable to undo it or recover the cash or the crypto, including a transfer to the wrong person. A finished send is not a Crypto.com refund.
  • Crypto.com scam. The app is the real company. The scam is the person who had you use it. Crypto.com’s published list says they will never send you a link to install software on your device, and they will never remotely access your account or device. The same page describes the pitches that actually take the money: an investment with high returns, a romance that moves to a private chat and then asks for crypto, and the version that does both, where you buy on a legitimate app and then send. That send is the case. Do not type a seed phrase for anyone claiming to be support, and do not send one to us.
  • Crypto.com withdrawal. Still inside the app: a review or a limit, read in the app you installed from the store, or on crypto.com typed by you. Already gone: the withdrawal says completed and the address is not yours. A “withdrawal pending” screen on a lookalike site is not a Crypto.com withdrawal.
Where the money actually is

The buy in the app and the send after it are different

Buying on Crypto.com and then sending to a manager, a romantic contact, or a wallet they named is the path their own scam page describes. The purchase can be a real Crypto.com purchase. The loss is the send. Their help treats that transfer as final.

A drain after remote-access software, or after a phrase typed into a page, used the account’s own send button. Save the transaction ID and the message. Reach support the way their page says: chat.crypto.com or the in-app chat, opened by you. Do not send the password, the codes, or the phrase to the person offering the refund.

A withdrawal that is still inside Crypto.com is their queue. A withdrawal that already says completed, to an address you do not recognise, is the send. Bring that transaction ID.

Before you pay anyone to fix it

Signs the Crypto.com refund offer is the second theft

  • They send a link and tell you to install software.Crypto.com’s scam page says they will never do that.
  • They want remote access to the phone or the computer.The same page says they will never remotely access your account or device.
  • They want a fee in crypto before a Crypto.com refund.That fee is the second theft. Their help already says a completed crypto transfer cannot be refunded.
  • The site is a letter off crypto.com.Type the address yourself, or open the app you installed from the store.
  • The withdrawal already says completed and you do not recognise the address.That is a finished send. Save the transaction ID.
What to send us

The file that makes a case review possible

The date, and where the money is now

Say whether the coins are still in the Crypto.com app or already sent. A balance screenshot on its own is not the trail.

The transaction ID, or the bank reference

The ID and the approximate time if the crypto left. The card or bank reference if a payment you did not authorise never became a send. Do not include your password, your codes, or a seed phrase.

The message that led you to send

The text, the profile, the call or the chat, including the address they gave you. If someone is now offering to force the refund, include that pitch and do not pay it.

If the coins already moved, read how a crypto trace works. If someone wants a fee up front to force the refund, read how to tell that pitch from an investigation.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Can I get a Crypto.com refund?

Crypto.com’s scam guide says a cryptocurrency transfer to a third party cannot be cancelled, reversed, or refunded. Their Send article says a completed transfer is final and they cannot undo it or recover the cash or the crypto. What can still be reviewed is the transaction ID of that send, and a card or bank payment you did not authorise.

Is Crypto.com a scam?

No. Crypto.com is a real app and exchange. The scam is the person who tells you to buy there and send the coins out. Crypto.com says it will never send a link to install software and will never remotely access your account or device. Their own page names the investment pitch, the romance approach, and the combination of the two. A hold that is still inside the app is their process. The outbound send is the case.

Why is my Crypto.com withdrawal pending?

If it has not left the app, read the status in the app you installed yourself or on crypto.com typed by you. If the withdrawal says completed and the destination is not your address, the send is finished and their help treats it as final. The transaction ID and the date are what a case review uses.

Someone says they can force the Crypto.com refund if I pay first.

A fee in crypto, a password, or a seed phrase is the second theft. Crypto.com has already published that a completed transfer cannot be refunded. Do not pay the fee. Send the transaction ID, the date, and the pitch.
