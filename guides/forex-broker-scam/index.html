Forex Broker Scam: How Fake Platforms Steal Deposits — and the Signs Most Victims Miss

A professional-looking platform, a friendly account manager, a first withdrawal that works — then the door slams shut. Every forex broker scam, every case of crypto exchange fraud and every reborn binary options scam runs the same basic machine. Here is how it works and how to check any platform before your money touches it.

Red flags

The warning signs of a forex broker scam

  • No verifiable licence: the site names a regulator but no licence number — or a number that belongs to a different, dissolved company.
  • A bonus or “managed account” that locks your deposit behind conditions you never agreed to.
  • Profits that only exist on the platform's own screen — no withdrawal to your own bank or wallet has ever succeeded.
  • A platform built this year claiming a decade of history; a domain a few months old wearing an “established broker” suit.
  • Cold contact: you were called, messaged or matched with an “advisor” who found you — you never found them.
Same machine

Crypto exchange fraud and binary options scams: new wrappers, same engine

The platform type barely matters. A binary options scam promised one-click bets with fixed payouts; today's fake exchanges promise AI trading, arbitrage bots or guaranteed-yield staking. Underneath, the engine is identical: deposits flow in, a dashboard fabricates growth, and every withdrawal attempt hits a fee, a tax or a silence.

That is why a checklist built for forex detects crypto exchange fraud just as reliably — the anatomy is the operator's behaviour, not the product name.

Before you deposit

The unregulated forex brokers list: five checks that take ten minutes

  • Check the register, not the website.Every serious regulator publishes a public register; a real licence number resolves to the exact company and domain you are using.
  • Read the withdrawal terms first.Search the fine print for bonus conditions and withdrawal fees — the scam's entire business model lives in that paragraph.
  • Test a small withdrawal early.A broker that delays your first $100 withdrawal will not pay your $10,000 one.
  • Search the name plus “withdrawal”.Victims of a forex broker scam report in predictable places; a clean search on a heavily marketed brand is itself a warning sign.
  • Check the domain's age.A broker “founded in 2010” on a domain registered last spring is telling you which part is real.
If it already happened

How to spot a fake crypto exchange — and what to do if one took your deposit

How to spot a fake crypto exchange in minutes

Real exchanges have verifiable corporate registrations, published wallets or proof-of-reserves, functioning support and years of organic reviews. Fake ones have a countdown timer on the signup bonus, a WhatsApp “support” line, and a withdrawal page that always needs one more payment first.

Stop feeding the platform

Whatever the next fee is called — tax, clearing, unlock — paying it only deepens the loss. Real platforms deduct fees from your balance; they never require new money to release your own.

Preserve, report, trace

Screenshot the dashboard, every chat and every email; file a report with your national cybercrime unit and the regulator the platform pretends to be regulated by; then have the crypto trail traced professionally — deposits to a fake exchange end at real exchanges, and that is where recovery begins.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is this a fake trading platform? How can I verify a broker's licence in ten minutes?

Find the licence number on the broker's site, then type it into the public register of the named regulator (FCA, CySEC, ASIC, BaFin, CONSOB or your national equivalent). The register entry must match the company name and the exact domain you use. A number that belongs to another firm — or to a dissolved one — is itself the scam's confession.

Are all unregulated brokers scams?

No — but every withdrawal-fraud operation we document is unregulated, because without a licence there is no regulator to complain to and no compensation scheme to claim against. Regulation is not a guarantee of honesty; the absence of it removes every safety net you would otherwise have.

I deposited with a binary options scam years ago — is recovery still possible?

Sometimes. Binary options fraud was run by boiler-room networks whose operators, payment processors and money mules remain traceable years later. The age of the loss narrows the bank-recall route but not the legal one; a documented evidence file is still worth building.
