Coinbase Refund: Scam, or a Withdrawal That Never Landed

People type three searches for the same afternoon: coinbase refund, coinbase scam, coinbase withdrawal. Coinbase is a real exchange, publicly traded on Nasdaq as COIN. A refund from Coinbase, a scam that used the name, and a withdrawal that never arrived are three different files. Coinbase's own help says an on-chain send cannot be cancelled, and that Coinbase cannot retrieve funds sent to the wrong address or the wrong network. The file that can still be reviewed is the one where the coins left for someone else, or a bank payment you did not authorise is still inside a dispute window.

Which search is yours

Coinbase refund, Coinbase scam, Coinbase withdrawal

  • Coinbase refund. Coinbase's help on sending says on-chain sends are irreversible, and Coinbase cannot retrieve funds sent to the wrong address or the wrong network. A send to an email, a phone number or a username is the exception they publish: if that person has no Coinbase account and does not claim the crypto within 30 days, the funds return, and a transfer that is still pending can be cancelled. A completed send to a crypto address is not a refund Coinbase can issue.
  • Coinbase scam. The exchange is the real company. Our review of Coinbase is the company check. The scam is the person using the name. Coinbase's own security page says staff will not ask for your password, your 2-step code or a seed phrase, will not ask you to install remote-access software, will not ask you to send crypto to an outside address, and will not call you to troubleshoot the account. A caller who does any of those is the theft.
  • Coinbase withdrawal. Two stalls share the words. Still inside the account: a review, a limit, or a bank that sent the cash back. That is Coinbase's own process, and the page that counts is one you opened by typing coinbase.com. Already gone: the withdrawal says completed and the address is not yours. That completed send is the file. A “withdrawal pending” screen on a site that is not coinbase.com is not a Coinbase withdrawal.
Where the money actually is

The buy on Coinbase and the send after it are different

Buying bitcoin or USDT on Coinbase and then sending it to a manager, a romantic contact, or a “safe wallet” is the usual path. The purchase can be a real Coinbase purchase. The loss is the send after it. Coinbase's help treats that on-chain send as final. A Coinbase refund of the purchase does not unwind a transfer you approved to someone else's address.

A drain after a code, a remote-access session, or a seed phrase used the account's own withdrawal tools. Save the transaction hash and the message. Lock the account from a page you typed yourself, and write to security@coinbase.com, which is the address on Coinbase's impersonation page. Do not send the password, the codes, or the phrase to the person offering to get the refund.

A cash withdrawal that is still sitting inside Coinbase is their queue or the receiving bank. It is not a trail we can follow, because the coins have not left. A withdrawal that already says completed, to an address you do not recognise, is the send. Bring that hash.

Before you pay anyone to fix it

Signs the Coinbase refund offer is the second theft

  • They tell you to move the balance to a new address “for safety”.Coinbase's own page says staff will not ask you to send crypto to an external address.
  • They ask for the password, the 2-step code, or a seed phrase.Coinbase says support does not ask for those. Typing them hands over the account.
  • They want a fee in crypto before a Coinbase refund.That fee is the second theft. It finds people searching this exact page.
  • The link is not coinbase.com.Type the address yourself. A letter off, or a form on another site, is the theft.
  • The withdrawal already says completed and you do not recognise the address.That is a finished send. Save the hash. Do not “reverse” it by sending a further payment.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Can I get a Coinbase refund?

Coinbase's own help says an on-chain send cannot be cancelled, and that Coinbase cannot retrieve funds sent to the wrong address or the wrong network. The published exception is a send to an email, phone number or username that the recipient never claims: if they have no Coinbase account, Coinbase says the funds return after 30 days, and a transfer that is still pending can be cancelled. A completed send to a crypto address is a different file. What can still be reviewed is the hash of that send, and a card or bank payment you did not authorise.

Is Coinbase a scam?

No. Coinbase is a real exchange, publicly traded on Nasdaq as COIN. The scam around the brand is impersonation. Coinbase's security page says staff will not ask for your password, your 2-step code or a seed phrase, will not ask you to install remote software, will not ask you to send crypto to an outside address, and will not call you to troubleshoot the account. A locked account or a slow cash withdrawal is their own process, opened at coinbase.com. A stranger who had you buy there and send the coins out is the case.

Why is my Coinbase withdrawal stuck?

If it has not left the account, it is Coinbase's review, a limit, or the bank, and the status that counts is the one on coinbase.com typed by you. If the withdrawal says completed and the destination is not your address, the send is finished. Coinbase's help treats that transfer as final. The hash and the date are what a case review uses.

Someone says they can force the Coinbase refund if I pay first.

A fee in crypto, a password, or a seed phrase is the second theft. Coinbase has already published that they cannot reverse a completed on-chain send. Do not pay the fee and do not send the login. Send the hash, the date, and the pitch.
