Route by route

How long each option stays open

Chargeback. Up to ~120 days via reason codes; some scenarios extend it. Filing within the window preserves your rights even while the case is investigated.

Wire recall. Day-one urgency: the receiving balance is the real deadline.

Ombudsman / bank complaint. Usually months after the bank's final response — a second chance with fresh eyes.

Civil claims. Years under limitation statutes, but evidence cools and assets move — earlier is stronger.

Criminal reports. Effectively always possible; they power freezing requests that no civil route matches.

The panic questions

What victims type at 2 a.m.

  • How long do I have to file a chargeback?Typically up to 120 days from the transaction or discovery — check your card network's reason codes; filing early beats filing perfectly.
  • Time limit to recover stolen funds?There is no single limit: chargebacks are months, recalls are days, civil claims are years. The practical rule: the free, fast routes expire first — use them in that order.
  • Is it too late to recover my money?Only a case review can answer — but if any of these is true, it is not too late: the fraud surfaced recently, crypto moved to an exchange, the bank never gave a final response, or a regulator warning exists. Each is an open door.
If a window has closed

The routes that survive expired deadlines

Civil claims outlive chargeback windows

Limitation periods usually run in years, not months — a lawyer or small-claims filing can still reach payment beneficiaries long after card windows shut.

Criminal reporting never expires

Police reports trigger exchange freezing and interbank flags years later; they also document your case for every other route.

Act this week, whatever the dates

Retrieval odds decay with time even where legal windows stay open — tracing, recalls and disputes all work better on fresh trails.

If the card window is still alive, the chargeback guide shows how to use it; for bank transfers, the wire guide covers the recall clock; and the legal routes guide explains the longer civil timelines.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How long do I have to file a chargeback?

Card networks generally allow up to 120 days, counted from the transaction — and for several reason codes from when you discovered the fraud or expected the service. Recent discovery can put an old deposit back inside the window; the only way to know is to file before the conservative deadline.

What is the statute of limitations for investment fraud?

It varies by jurisdiction and claim type — typically one to six years for civil fraud claims, often longer for criminal matters. The legal window is rarely the binding constraint; evidence quality and asset location are, which is why fast action beats perfect preparation.

Is it too late to recover my money?

Not necessarily — and here is the test: did the fraud surface recently (chargeback may apply), did crypto land at an exchange (freezing is possible), did your bank never issue a final response (ombudsman route open), or does a regulator warning exist (enforcement leverage)? Any 'yes' means time remains.
