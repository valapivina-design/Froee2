The machinery

How clone apps and white-label platforms work

The shell. A rented trading interface — sometimes a familiar-looking trading server, sometimes a web app with a chart widget — with the broker's logo applied.

The feed. Prices copied from public data, delayed or shifted against you; 'slippage' that only ever works in one direction.

The wallet. Deposits go to a personal account, a shell company or a crypto address — never to a segregated client-money account.

The back office. Staff watch your balance and your intent: withdraw small and it may pay, to keep you hooked; withdraw large and the block begins.

Clone-app red flags

How to tell the app from the real thing

  • No listing on the official App Store / Play Store — a fake trading app no name can hide behind.Real finance apps pass store review under a registered operator; with a fake trading app no name is only the start — the licence and the balance are faked too. Clones live on direct-download links and 'enterprise' installs.
  • A name that mimics a known platform with one letter changed.Clone branding rides on trust it did not earn — check every character of the name and domain.
  • Profits that only go up.Markets have losing days; a balance that climbs steadily is an admin panel, not a portfolio.
  • Withdrawal requires 'tax' or 'unlock fees' first.Regulated brokers deduct costs from the withdrawal — they never demand prepaid release fees.
  • Support only inside the app or via WhatsApp.Real platforms have verifiable, regulated support channels — not a chat window the operator can delete.
Wallet hack or fake exchange?

The distinction that changes your recovery route

If your real wallet was drained

That is a wallet hack: keys or seed phrases were compromised. The route is blockchain tracing of the stolen funds, exchange freezing requests and police reports — speed matters most.

If you deposited into a platform

That is a fake exchange: you sent funds willingly to a fraudster's address. The route is card/bank disputes on fiat deposits, tracing of crypto through the laundering chain, and building the evidence file that regulators and banks accept.

Preserve the app itself

Screenshots of every screen, the APK or the web app's URL, wallet addresses it showed you. The app is the primary exhibit — forensic analysis of it regularly reveals the operator's infrastructure.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How do I check if a trading app is real?

Three checks in five minutes: is it listed under its exact name on the official App Store or Play Store; does the operator behind it appear in a regulator's register with a matching licence number; and does a withdrawal of a small amount complete without conditions. Failing any one is a stop signal.

Is a white-label forex broker always a scam?

No — white-label technology is common and legal. It becomes fraud when the operator has no licence, client money goes to personal accounts, or the white-label provider's due diligence was a fiction. The label matters less than the licence and the money trail.

My wallet shows a transfer I never made — hack or scam?

An outgoing transaction you never authorised indicates a compromised wallet (hack). A platform balance you can see but never withdraw indicates a fake exchange. Both are recoverable in part, but through different doors — preserve evidence before doing anything else.
