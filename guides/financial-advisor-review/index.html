Financial Advisor Review — Is This Financial Advisor a Scam?

This financial advisor review is of the pattern, not of one postcode. A real adviser sits on a public register you type yourself. A financial advisor scam borrows a name, a photo and a licence number, then asks for a transfer in chat. We investigate those files as a cybersecurity company — we do not replace the bank, the card scheme, or the police.

The pattern

What a financial advisor scam actually looks like

  • Contact on WhatsApp, Telegram or LinkedIn — not a number you found on the register.
  • A 'firm' whose licence number belongs to someone else, or to a dissolved company.
  • A pitch for a pension transfer, an ISA, a bond or a 'private' fund that can only be paid to a personal account or a wallet.
  • Urgency: the window closes today; the manager is in another country; you must not tell your bank the true purpose.
  • A recovery room that appears the week you complain.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is this financial advisor a scam?

A name on a register you typed, paid into a client account named there, is one file. A WhatsApp 'adviser' and a personal transfer is a financial advisor scam pattern. We investigate the payment. We do not impersonate the firm.

They used a real company's name. Is the company guilty?

Usually not. Impersonation is the product. The real firm often publishes a warning. Keep the chat. That is the file.

My adviser has a different title — wealth manager, IFA, planner. Does this still apply?

Yes. This page is the category. Send the date, how you paid, and the name they used.
