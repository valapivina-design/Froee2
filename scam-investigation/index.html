Why standard banks and lawyers struggle to recover your funds

Recovering money after an online scam is a technical problem before it is a legal one. Here's where the usual routes hit a wall.

The "black box" problem

Most banks see a transaction, not a scam. When money moves via Wise, Revolut or crypto, they often call it "final settlement" — because they lack the tools to trace the beneficiary beyond the first hop.

The "anonymous" lie

Scammers hide behind shell companies, throwaway domains and temporary emails. A standard lawyer can't tie a WhatsApp number or a crypto wallet to a real person in Germany, Austria or beyond.

The proof gap

Banks and courts need evidence, not your word. The questions that actually move a case are technical:

  • Who really owns the domain?
  • Where is the server located?
  • Who is the real beneficiary of the crypto wallet?
  • Is the "broker" a real person or a call centre abroad?

How we investigate online scams using technology

A multi-layered, digital-forensics approach — every finding sourced, timestamped and documented for your dossier.

1

Digital footprint analysis — who are they?

We establish who is really behind the platform or profile.

  • Domain age & registration: was the platform created before your complaint, or days ago? Scam sites are often under six months old.
  • WHOIS data: we trace the registrant's name, address and email, cross-referencing to see past privacy-shield redaction where possible.
  • Social-media forensics: we examine the "broker's" LinkedIn, Facebook and Instagram — do the photos, history and numbers hold up, or point to a fake?
2

Financial traceability — where is the money?

We follow the funds across the rails they moved over.

  • Crypto blockchain analysis: we trace Bitcoin, Ethereum and USDT from your wallet onward, identifying the exchanges and high-risk jurisdictions the funds reach.
  • Bank transfer mapping: we analyse SWIFT/IBAN details — is the receiving bank in a known fraud hub, and does the account name match the platform?
  • Payment-gateway tracing: for Wise, Revolut and PayPal, we work to identify the underlying account and the beneficiary's country.
3

Evidence compilation — the dossier

We turn the findings into a single, professional case file.

  • Expert report: all findings compiled into one clear PDF dossier.
  • Court-ready format: structured for submission to banks, courts and prosecutors.
  • Visual money-flow maps: wallet → exchange → bank account, at a glance.

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers about online-scam investigations.

Can you trace a Bitcoin wallet to a person?

Bitcoin is pseudonymous, so we trace a wallet's transactions to the exchange or service where the funds land — points that hold KYC records. We document that trail so law enforcement or a court can compel the identity. We do not hack accounts or obtain private data unlawfully.

Is your report admissible in court?

We prepare each dossier to evidentiary standards — timestamps, methodology and verifiable sources — so it's ready for submission to banks, prosecutors and courts. Whether evidence is ultimately admitted is always a decision for the court or authority handling your case.

How long does an investigation take?

A simple bank trace is typically 24–48 hours, a crypto trace 3–7 days, and a complex multi-layered scam 1–2 weeks, depending on the hops and jurisdictions involved.

Do you guarantee my money back?

No. No investigator can guarantee recovery — it depends on whether the funds still exist and are reachable. What we provide is strong, well-documented evidence that improves your chances of a successful chargeback, complaint or legal action.
