The short answer

The short answer: the real Michael Saylor is not a scammer — scammers are stealing their identity

Non-defamation notice: Michael Saylor is a real, respected public figure with no connection to the schemes described on this page. Nothing here alleges any wrongdoing by Michael Saylor — the only criminals are the impersonators, and this page exists to protect both their victims and the person whose identity is being stolen.

What checks out about the real person:

  • Michael Saylor is the executive chairman of Strategy (formerly MicroStrategy) and one of the largest publicly known individual Bitcoin holders — a real, verifiable public figure with no connection to any giveaway scheme.
  • Saylor’s own team reports removing roughly 80 AI-generated deepfake videos of him every single day (Newsweek, 17 January 2024) — he is one of the most-impersonated people in crypto, and a victim of these schemes, not a perpetrator.

What the impersonation fraud looks like — every item below is documented:

  • 17 January 2022 — a victim lost $1.14 million (26.4 BTC) to scammers posing as Saylor in a fake YouTube giveaway routed through a site called “ms22” — reported as the largest single payment to a fake giveaway at that time (Yahoo Finance / Whale Alert).
  • A 2024 academic analysis of a January 2023 campaign documents the fake MicroStrategy site mxstrx2.com, promising a $1B giveaway (1,000 BTC + 10,000 ETH) through a hijacked YouTube livestream, with named BTC and ETH receiving addresses.
  • 17 January 2024 — Newsweek reports FTC social-media scam losses of $2.7B, an $800 loss to a fake Saylor deepfake giveaway, and Saylor’s team removing ~80 deepfake videos of him daily.
  • 19 January 2024 — HackRead / Bitdefender document YouTube stream-jacking campaigns using deepfakes of Saylor, Musk and Garlinghouse with QR codes; one tracked campaign raked in at least $600K.
  • TRM Labs (July 2025) reports generative-AI scam reports up 456% year-on-year, with the deepfake crypto giveaway the most commonly reported AI-enabled scam type and Saylor among the most-impersonated figures; the SEC has issued an investor alert on impersonation of investment professionals.

If you were contacted by a "Michael Saylor" pitching crypto, you were talking to an impersonator. Preserve every message, URL, wallet address and transaction hash — then start with a free case review below.

Victim reports

If this happened to you, you were targeted by an impersonator — not by Michael Saylor

These are the patterns victims of the fake "Michael Saylor" schemes describe:

  • watched a YouTube, Facebook or TikTok livestream “featuring” Saylor offering to double BTC or ETH sent to a displayed address or QR code
  • sent crypto and received nothing back
  • the stream, site or channel disappeared shortly after the payment
  • in follow-up variants, was asked for a “withdrawal fee” or “tax” to release the doubled funds
  • assumed Saylor himself was involved — he was not

None of this involves the real Michael Saylor. It involves criminals wearing their identity — and that distinction matters, because it is the impersonators' money trail that we investigate.
