What checks out

marcus.com is the real bank — send proof if a fake, an impersonator, or a technical block took the money

Marcus by Goldman Sachs at marcus.com is a real US bank deposit brand. These are the public facts. They do not describe a trading broker.

  • Goldman Sachs Bank USA, FDIC Certificate 33124, lists www.marcus.com as its primary website on the FDIC BankFind record. The bank has been FDIC-insured since 28 November 2008, and its primary federal regulator is the Federal Reserve.
  • Marcus states that its savings and CD accounts are deposit products of Goldman Sachs Bank USA, Salt Lake City Branch. FDIC insurance follows the usual rule: up to $250,000 per depositor, per ownership category, at that bank, and other Goldman Sachs Bank USA deposits count toward the same limit.
  • The products published on marcus.com are an online savings account and CDs. Rates are posted on the site and can change. This is a US bank deposit brand, not a forex or crypto broker.

Three losses are a different file from a normal Marcus savings account. A technical excuse: the screen showed an error, a locked withdrawal, or a fee to “fix” it. Impersonation: someone contacted you as Marcus or Goldman and told you where to pay. A fake broker: a trading site used the Marcus name, and the domain was not marcus.com. Send the domain you actually used, the date, how you paid, and one screenshot. We investigate that file. We are not the bank, and the first look is free.

Sort your situation

Real-platform dispute or impersonation fraud?

Both feel identical from the inside. They are fixed by completely different routes — and the fastest way to tell them apart is to ask three questions.

If it is the real platform: your dispute runs through its formal complaints process, then the regulator or ombudsman. Slow, but it usually works. If you typed marcus.com yourself, a dispute with the real bank goes through Goldman Sachs Bank USA, and you can still send us the transfer record. If someone contacted you first, the domain was different, or a technical error asked for a new payment, treat it as impersonation or a fake broker. Goldman Financial at gogoldman.com is not Marcus.

If it was an impersonator: you were never a customer of Marcus at all. A clone site, a fake “account manager”, a lookalike domain — the money went to criminals, not to the platform. That is fraud, and speed decides what can be recovered.

The three questions: did you type the official domain yourself, or follow a link, advert or message? Did anyone contact you first? Were you asked to pay a personal account or a different domain? If any answer is wrong, treat it as impersonation — and start here.
