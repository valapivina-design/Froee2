The short answer

The short answer: the real MrBeast is not a scammer — scammers are stealing their identity

Non-defamation notice: MrBeast is a real, respected public figure with no connection to the schemes described on this page. Nothing here alleges any wrongdoing by MrBeast — the only criminals are the impersonators, and this page exists to protect both their victims and the person whose identity is being stolen.

What checks out about the real person:

  • MrBeast is Jimmy Donaldson, a YouTube creator known for large public giveaways and challenges. He is not a broker, he does not cold-message strangers, and a real giveaway does not require you to deposit crypto to withdraw a prize.
  • No public record makes him the operator of a trading platform. A video, comment or ad that says otherwise is the impersonation.

What the impersonation fraud looks like — every item below is documented:

  • Which? documented YouTube ads copying MrBeast's branding, promising cash such as $1,000 to everyone who visits a page, then sending people to phishing sites that collect personal data or push malware and a PayPal email.
  • Tenable documented fake MrBeast YouTube ads pointing at cashtab.info, with a cartoon likeness, a claimed $500 reserved reward, and a survey wall. The site's own fine print said it was not affiliated with MrBeast — after using his likeness to get the click.
  • Norton described TikTok deepfakes of MrBeast and others promising about $8,000 in Bitcoin that appears in a fake account, then asking for a $300 to $500 deposit before any withdrawal. The sites rotate.
  • BBB Scam Tracker 1223966 (16 March 2026, victim account): a post claiming MrBeast was launching a cryptocurrency and giving away $2,500, then a $200 deposit to “verify” and $300 more to withdraw, at Vyroso.win and Vyroso777.to. Reported loss $200.

If you were contacted by a "MrBeast" pitching crypto, you were talking to an impersonator. Preserve every message, URL, wallet address and transaction hash — then start with a free case review below.

Victim reports

If this happened to you, you were targeted by an impersonator — not by MrBeast

These are the patterns victims of the fake "MrBeast" schemes describe:

  • a YouTube, TikTok or Facebook ad of MrBeast promising cash or Bitcoin
  • a prize that appears in an account only after you deposit
  • a survey, a wallet “verification”, or a fee before the giveaway is paid
  • a site that disappears, or a second fee, once you have paid

None of this involves the real MrBeast. It involves criminals wearing their identity — and that distinction matters, because it is the impersonators' money trail that we investigate.
