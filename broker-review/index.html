How we review

Not star ratings — forensic verification

A typical broker review tells you about spreads and platforms. Ours answers the question victims actually search: can this broker be trusted with my money, and what do I do if it already has it? Every review on this page is built from public regulatory records, formal regulator warnings, domain and corporate tracing, and the documented patterns we see in active investigations.

If a platform you used appears here — or if your broker is blocking withdrawals, demanding "taxes" before payout, or has simply gone silent — that situation is investigable. The reviews below show what we found; the scam investigation service is what we do about it.

Our method

What we check before calling anything a review

  • Licence reality. Every claimed licence is checked on the regulator's own register — FCA, CySEC, ASIC, SEC, CFTC, BaFin — by name, number and legal entity.
  • Corporate identity. WHOIS history, company registrations, directors and addresses — does the platform exist where it claims to exist?
  • Warning lists. Formal alerts from the FCA, SEC, CFTC, ASIC, CySEC and CNMV, plus consumer complaint patterns.
  • Money trail readiness. Deposit addresses, payment rails and domain infrastructure — the evidence that makes an investigation possible.
  • Withdrawal behaviour. The single most reliable scam signal: can clients actually get their money out?
FAQ

Broker review FAQ

How do I check if a broker is a scam?

Three steps take under ten minutes: search the firm's exact name and licence number on the relevant regulator's register (never trust a logo on the broker's own site); check the regulator's warning list for clones and unauthorised firms; and test a small withdrawal before committing serious funds. Failing any one of the three is a stop signal — our eToro review shows the method applied step by step.

My broker won't let me withdraw — is that a scam?

Withdrawal blocking is the single most common scam pattern: "taxes" or "fees" demanded before payout, "account managers" pressuring more deposits, sudden verification loops, or complete silence. It can also occasionally be a compliance delay at a legitimate firm. The difference is evidence — preserve every message and payment record, and have the situation assessed before paying anything more.

Can you investigate a broker that isn't on your review list?

Yes. The reviews here are platforms we've assessed proactively; the investigation service applies to any broker, exchange or investment platform. If you were fed to this page by a search about a specific platform, that platform is exactly what we can look into.

Can I get my money back from a scam broker?

Sometimes, and speed matters. Card and bank transfers can occasionally be recalled; crypto trails can be traced to exchanges where the funds cash out. No honest firm guarantees recovery — anyone who does is usually running a recovery scam. What an investigation gives you is a documented trail that banks, police, regulators and courts can act on.

Are your broker reviews paid or affiliated?

No. These reviews are forensic assessments based on public regulatory records and documented investigation patterns. Where the evidence says scam, we show the record; where a company is real but the complaints are stacking up, we say that too — and we investigate. The reviews exist to route victims to competent investigation, not to sell broker referrals.

Suspect your broker?

Withdrawals blocked, account frozen, or a "broker" you can no longer reach — tell us what happened. The sooner the money trail is preserved, the more can be done.
