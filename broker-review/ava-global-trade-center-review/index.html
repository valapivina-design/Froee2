The short answer

AvaTrade is real and regulated — “AVA Global Trade Center” is not AvaTrade

First, the good news — the real brand checks out:

  • AvaTrade (avatrade.com) is one of the most licensed retail brokers in the industry — Central Bank of Ireland, CySEC, ASIC, FSA Japan, FSCA, FSRA and BVI FSC all on record.
  • The impersonation ecosystem is active and documented: the FCA separately warned (30 Apr 2026) about an AvaTrade clone “Ava+trade” (avaplustrade.co.uk) — this brand family is hunted constantly.

Now the bad news — the clones:

  • avaglobaltradecenter.com sits behind a bot-verification wall with no retrievable corporate detail — transparency is the first thing a regulated broker provides.
  • BrokersView's Q&A context (27 Sep 2026) places the brand in clone-impersonation territory: not recommended, no verifiable registration.
  • The name stacks “AVA” + “Global” + “Trade Center” to intercept AvaTrade searches — brand-confusion harvesting, the clone economy's core mechanic.

If the site you used is not the official domain — avatrade.com — it was not AVA Global Trade Center. It was someone wearing its name.

Sort your situation

Real-platform dispute or impersonation fraud?

Both feel identical from the inside. They are fixed by completely different routes — and the fastest way to tell them apart is to ask three questions.

If it is the real platform: your dispute runs through its formal complaints process, then the regulator or ombudsman. Slow, but it usually works. Verify every AvaTrade-branded domain against avatrade.com before typing credentials or sending money. If a “regional” or “global” AvaTrade site approached you first — by ad, message or “account manager” — treat it as hostile until proven otherwise on the official domain.

If it was an impersonator: you were never a customer of AVA Global Trade Center at all. A clone site, a fake “account manager”, a lookalike domain — the money went to criminals, not to the platform. That is fraud, and speed decides what can be recovered.

The three questions: did you type the official domain yourself, or follow a link, advert or message? Did anyone contact you first? Were you asked to pay a personal account or a different domain? If any answer is wrong, treat it as impersonation — and start here.

Is AVA Global Trade Center a scam?

The real AvaTrade, no — it is a long-established, multi-regulated broker at avatrade.com. AVA Global Trade Center, yes: it is a lookalike name with no AvaTrade affiliation, no retrievable company detail and a bot wall where its disclosures should be. Is AVA Global Trade Center a legit broker?

The real AVA Global Trade Center is verifiable — the genuine AvaTrade group (avatrade.com) — regulated by the Central Bank of Ireland, CySEC, ASIC, FSA Japan, FSCA, FSRA and BVI FSC.
