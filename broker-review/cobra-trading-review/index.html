The short answer: Cobra Trading is real — but read this before you hand over any money

Cobra Trading is a US direct-access broker for active equity and options traders (Plano, Texas, est. 2003–2004) — SEC-registered, FINRA and SIPC member, clearing through Wedbush — the regulated baseline this site's offshore lookalikes are measured against These are the facts that check out on public registers — the complaint record is what our review is still working through:

Sort your situation

Real-platform dispute or impersonation fraud?

Both feel identical from the inside. They are fixed by completely different routes — and the fastest way to tell them apart is to ask three questions.

If it is the real platform: your dispute runs through its formal complaints process, then the regulator or ombudsman. Slow, but it usually works. If you are comparing offshore “brokers” against a US-regulated baseline, this is the baseline: SEC registration, FINRA membership, SIPC cover, real clearing arrangements. Anything offering the same product with none of the registrations is telling you what it is.

If it was an impersonator: you were never a customer of Cobra Trading at all. A clone site, a fake “account manager”, a lookalike domain — the money went to criminals, not to the platform. That is fraud, and speed decides what can be recovered.

The three questions: did you type the official domain yourself, or follow a link, advert or message? Did anyone contact you first? Were you asked to pay a personal account or a different domain? If any answer is wrong, treat it as impersonation — and start here.
