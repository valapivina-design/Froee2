Sort your situation

Real-platform dispute or impersonation fraud?

Both feel identical from the inside. They are fixed by completely different routes — and the fastest way to tell them apart is to ask three questions.

If it is the real platform: your dispute runs through its formal complaints process, then the regulator or ombudsman. Slow, but it usually works. Recurring publisher complaints concern account bans at withdrawal time over “traffic quality” disputes, and occasional advertiser complaints about low-quality traffic — contract disputes, not consumer fraud. An “advertise with Bitmedia” pitch that asks you to invest in an ad “package” is not this company.

If it was an impersonator: you were never a customer of Bitmedia at all. A clone site, a fake “account manager”, a lookalike domain — the money went to criminals, not to the platform. That is fraud, and speed decides what can be recovered.

The three questions: did you type the official domain yourself, or follow a link, advert or message? Did anyone contact you first? Were you asked to pay a personal account or a different domain? If any answer is wrong, treat it as impersonation — and start here.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is Bitmedia a scam?

No — Bitmedia is a real business: UK-registered BITMEDIA LTD (incorporated 2015, active), with named case studies, 550+ publishers and a long track record corroborated by third-party reviews and Bitcointalk threads since 2015–2016. The scam stories you see online usually trace to fakes borrowing its name — and the ones that don't are part of our active investigation.

Is Bitmedia regulated?

As an advertising network it does not require FCA authorisation. Its footprint is verifiable through its UK-registered company (BITMEDIA LTD, active since 2015) and a decade of operating history.

I lost money to someone claiming to be Bitmedia — is that the real company?

Almost certainly not. Impersonators borrow the name, the logo, even the licence number. The real company knows nothing about it. Keep every message, receipt, URL and wallet address — that evidence is what an investigation builds on.

Can I recover money lost to a fake Bitmedia site?

Sometimes. Bank and card transfers can occasionally be recalled if you move fast, and crypto leaves a traceable trail. No honest investigator guarantees recovery. What we can give you is a documented, court-usable trail — start with a free case review.

How do I check the Bitmedia site I used is genuine?

Type the official domain yourself — bitmedia.io — never follow a link from an advert, email or message. Then check the regulator's public register, which lists the firm's genuine website. If your domain differs by even one character, it was a fake.
